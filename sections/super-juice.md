# Super Juice

Super juice is a bartending trick for getting roughly **eight times** as much usable citrus out of the same fruit. Instead of squeezing limes and lemons and tossing the peels, you steep the peels in citric (and, for lime, malic) acid to pull an *oleo citrate* from the oils, then blend that with water and the squeezed juice. The result keeps far longer than fresh juice and tastes remarkably close to it.

The method and proportions here come from **Kevin Kos** — see his writeup, [How to get 8x as much juice from one citrus](https://www.kevinkos.com/post/how-to-get-8x-as-much-juice-from-one-citrus), and his [super juice calculator](https://www.kevinkos.com/super-juice-calculator-1). The calculator below is our own, built so the quantities land right inside the steps.

## Proportions

Everything is measured against the **weight of the peels**:

- **Lime** — citric acid at 0.66× the peel weight, malic acid at 0.33×, and water at 16.66×.
- **Lemon** — citric acid at 1.0× the peel weight and water at 16.66× (no malic).

## Our 1:1 dilution

Undiluted super juice is more concentrated than we like for easy mixing, so we **cut the finished juice 1:1 with water** — stir in an equal volume, doubling it. It softens the concentration to taste and, conveniently, doubles the yield for batching.

In practice the peels from **2 lemons or 3 limes** make about **2 cups** of base super juice, or roughly **4 cups** after the cut — plenty for a party. It keeps about a week or two refrigerated (Kos suggests using it within a week; in our experience it holds a little longer), and it freezes well: pour the extra into ice-cube trays and thaw a cube whenever you need fresh-tasting citrus on hand.

## Notes

Acid strengths and peel weights vary a little by fruit, so treat the calculator's output — especially the *# of fruit* estimate — as a starting point and adjust to taste.
